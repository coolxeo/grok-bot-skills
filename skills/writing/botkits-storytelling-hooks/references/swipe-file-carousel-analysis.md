## Findings

Counts are marked **[count]** and come from the tables above. Observations are marked **[obs]** and come from looking at the posts and images; they are judgment, not measurement.

1. **Bookmarks beat likes on reference posts.** [count] 10 of the 12 source posts have more bookmarks than likes (ratio above 1.0). The author's personal post sits at 0.08. A list people want to reuse gets saved, not applauded.
2. **Size of the list drives the outlier.** [count] Same author, same look, same one-line text: the 80-item, 11-slide post reached 186,583 impressions while his 4 to 8 item single images sat at a median of 3,126. [obs] A big round number on the cover ("80") reads as a whole library, not a tip.
3. **Multi-slide carousels save the most per view.** [count] The three multi-image posts have the three highest BM/1k in the table: 42.8 (9 slides), 38.1 (6 slides) and 34.4 (11 slides). The single-image posts range from 11.7 to 27.9. Small sample, so treat this as a lead, not a law.
4. **The post text is the headline and nothing more.** [obs] Posts 1, 8 and 11 carry only "[N] [Things] To [Outcome]:" ending in a colon. The colon points at the images. No link, no thread, no hashtag, no "bookmark this". [count] Post 1 has 37 replies against 6,425 bookmarks: people save and leave.
5. **Fill-in-the-blank items are tools, not tips.** [obs] Every item on the seed carousel is one sentence with bracketed blanks the reader fills in. That turns the slide into a template the reader will come back to, which is the reason to bookmark.
6. **One fixed grid on every slide.** [obs] Same top bar, same title block, same row count, same footer on all 10 content slides. The 9-slide and 6-slide posts do the same with their own layouts. The reader learns the grid on slide 2 and then just reads.
7. **Each slide is a named group with a "use when" line.** [obs] The seed post sorts 80 items into 10 named groups and gives each group one line on when to use it. The list becomes something you can look things up in, not a wall.
8. **Quiet, print-like design.** [obs] Paper-tone ground, dark ink, serif display, monospace labels, page counters, no stock photos, no emoji. It looks like a page torn from a good book, which suits a save-for-later post.
9. **Text-list variant (Ruben Hassid, Jack).** [obs] Posts 2, 3, 5, 6, 9 and 10 put the list in a long post body with one image (a screenshot or a dense table). They still save well (ratio 1.69 to 2.41). [count] Post 5 has 356 replies on 204 likes; the reply count suggests a "comment to get it" gate, but the replies were not read, so this is not verified.

## Formula, as a reusable shape

- **Post text:** "[N] [noun] to [outcome]:" with the outcome in the reader's words. One line, ends in a colon.
- **N:** a number big enough to feel like a library (the outlier used 80). Make it groups x items, with the same item count per slide.
- **Slide 1:** the number huge, the noun, the outcome small, a "swipe" cue, a page counter.
- **Slides 2 to N:** one named group per slide, one "use when" line, 6 to 8 numbered one-sentence items. Blanks marked so the item works as a template.
- **Last slide (optional):** who made it and where the long version lives. Keep links off the post itself.
- **Design:** one grid, one paper-tone ground, at most one accent, 3:4 portrait.

## What could not be verified

- Why the seed post says "/ 12" but carries 11 images.
- Whether post 5's 356 replies come from a comment-to-get gate (replies not read).
- Reach drivers outside the post: paid boosts, reposts by large accounts, or the time of day. The API reads show none of that.
- X search has no engagement filter, so the 12 posts are the best of about 225 candidates read across 9 searches, not a ranked sample of all of X.
