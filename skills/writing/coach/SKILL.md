# Coach

A developmental read. Find out whether the piece works before anyone fixes commas. Read-only: the writer keeps the pen.

## First, three questions

Ask only what the draft and chat do not already answer:

- Who is the reader, and what do they already know?
- Where will it run (X Article, LinkedIn, blog, newsletter, docs)?
- What should the reader do or believe afterwards?

The answers set the bar. A 300-word LinkedIn post is not judged like a 3,000-word essay.

## Gate check

Write a reverse outline: one line per paragraph saying what it does. Then answer yes or no:

1. **Point.** Can you say the thesis in one sentence? Write it down, or say why you can't.
2. **Order.** Does each paragraph need the one before it? Name the first place the chain breaks.
3. **Support.** Is every load-bearing claim backed by a fact, example, or source? List the gaps.

Any "no" ends the review here. Report the gate failures with the reverse outline and send the writer back to outline or draft. Scoring the voice of a broken piece wastes their time.

## Score (only if the gate passes)

1-5 each, where 3 is fine and 5 is ready to publish:

| Axis | What a 5 looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Entry | The first lines give a specific moment, number, or problem, not a warm-up. |
| Build | One line of argument that gains ground and lands. |
| Voice | Sounds like one person on one day. No inserted punchlines or AI tells (see no-ai-slop). |
| Evidence | The reader trusts the claims because they can see where they come from. |
| Takeaway | One sentence the reader could repeat to a friend. |
| Reach | Terms the target reader may not know are explained once, without talking down. |
| Pace | Sections connect by logic, not by transition words. Lengths vary with weight. |

## Report

- Gate result, then total out of 35 with one line per axis.
- What already works, quoted. Name it so the writer protects it.
- Top three problems, structural ones first, each with a quoted line and a before/after sketch.
- One question you would ask the writer in person.

Do not edit the file. Do not rewrite whole sections.

## Inspired by

- [Nadiem Mahmoud, coach](https://github.com/nadiem99/claude-writing-skills/blob/5b52111e1bd30e93643929f17155e82aa26f771f/skills/pipeline/coach/SKILL.md) (MIT), which credits Every's Eleanor Warnock. Does well: a structure-first gate before any scoring and a seven-axis rubric. Missing: tied to its own vault folders and brand-guide files, and it judges every piece by an essay arc that opens on a scene.
- [Paul Graham, Writing, Briefly](https://www.paulgraham.com/writing44.html). Does well: write for a reader who reads less carefully than you, and read drafts aloud to find the boring parts. Missing: a list of habits, with no review procedure or output.
- [George Orwell, Politics and the English Language](https://www.orwellfoundation.com/the-orwell-foundation/orwell/essays-and-other-works/politics-and-the-english-language/). Does well: insists on knowing exactly what you mean before choosing words. Missing: sentence-level rules for 1946 prose, nothing on structure or argument.

## What this version adds

- Reader, venue, and goal questions so the bar fits the format.
- A reverse outline as the gate evidence, so "structure broken" comes with proof.
- A "what works" section, and one in-person question for the writer.
