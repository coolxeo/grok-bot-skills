# Draft

Write from an approved outline, one section at a time, with the writer approving each before the next. Get a rough shape down, then improve it. Never produce the whole article in one go.

## Before each section

1. Say the thesis back in one sentence. If it has drifted or tries to say two things, stop and fix that first.
2. Name the section and why it comes next.
3. Name two habits of this writer's voice you will keep (first person, short punch paragraphs, dry asides, exact numbers). Use their earlier posts or notes if you have them.

## While writing

- Every paragraph moves the piece: a new fact, a new step, or a new turn. A paragraph that repeats the last one in other words gets cut.
- Plain, ordinary words. If a simpler word exists, use it.
- Open each section on something concrete. No "Have you ever wondered".
- Prose by default. Bullets only for steps or real lists.
- Mix short and long sentences on purpose.
- Start a sentence with "But" or "And" when it reads naturally. Grammar allows it.
- No invented numbers, quotes, or sources. Leave `[GAP: what is needed]` and keep going.
- Follow no-ai-slop's house rules as you write (no em dashes in short copy, no staged contrasts, no slogan endings). Cheaper than fixing them later.

## After each section

- Offer two versions of any line that carries a lot of weight (the hook, a key claim, the last line) and let the writer pick.
- Reread from the top. Look for tone shifts and points made twice.
- If the section is fighting the outline, say so and propose an outline change. Going back early beats polishing a broken arc.
- Stop and wait for approval unless the writer asked for more than one section.

When every section is in: source-check, then no-ai-slop.

## Inspired by

- [Nadiem Mahmoud, draft](https://github.com/nadiem99/claude-writing-skills/blob/5b52111e1bd30e93643929f17155e82aa26f771f/skills/pipeline/draft/SKILL.md) (MIT). Does well: section-by-section drafting, thesis check first, voice calibration, and going back to the outline when needed. Missing: bans sentences that start with "But" or "And", and leans on vault folders and context files the user may not have.
- [Paul Graham, Write Simply](https://www.paulgraham.com/simply.html) and [Writing, Briefly](https://www.paulgraham.com/writing44.html). Does well: ordinary words and a fast bad first version, then rewriting. Missing: no checkpoints or collaboration rules.
- [George Orwell, Politics and the English Language](https://www.orwellfoundation.com/the-orwell-foundation/orwell/essays-and-other-works/politics-and-the-english-language/). Does well: short rules with an escape clause for when a rule makes the sentence worse. Missing: nothing on structure or drafting with a collaborator.

## What this version adds

- Gap markers so drafting never stops for a missing fact and never invents one.
- Anti-slop house rules applied during drafting, not only after.
- Explicit permission for natural "But" and "And" openers.
